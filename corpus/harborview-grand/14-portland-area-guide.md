# Harborview Grand — Portland Area Guide

18 Wharf Street, Portland, ME 04101 · +1-207-555-0100
Last reviewed October 2026.

Walking times are from our Wharf Street entrance.

## The Old Port

We are in it. The Old Port is Portland's 19th-century working waterfront: cobbled streets, brick
warehouses turned into shops and restaurants, and a harbour that is still commercial rather than
ornamental. Most of what visitors come for is within ten minutes on foot.

Cobbles are uneven throughout. Commercial Street has the smoothest pavement and is the better
route for wheels of any kind.

## Getting on the water

**Casco Bay Lines** — 2 minutes. Ferries to the Calendar Islands, from a 20-minute hop to Peaks
Island to the three-hour mailboat run that stops at six islands. The mailboat is the single best
thing to do here on a clear day. Buy at the terminal; summer sailings sell out by mid-morning.

**Harbour cruises and charters** — from the wharves either side of us, roughly May to October.

## Eating

The Old Port is dense with restaurants and the good ones book out. Ask the front desk to call
ahead — a same-evening table is often possible at 5:30 PM when it is impossible at 7:30.

- **Seafood** — the wharves have the obvious options; Commercial Street has the famous ones.
- **Breakfast beyond ours** — several bakeries on Fore Street open at 7:00 AM.
- **Late** — kitchens in the Old Port generally close at 9:00 PM midweek, later at weekends. If
  you arrive on an evening ferry, eat before you unpack.

## Museums and sights

**Portland Museum of Art** — 12 minutes. Closed Mondays outside summer.

**Portland Observatory** — 20 minutes uphill on Munjoy Hill. The only surviving maritime signal
tower in the US; the climb is worth it for the harbour view.

**Portland Head Light** — 15 minutes by car, in Cape Elizabeth. The lighthouse everyone
photographs. No direct bus; taxi or rideshare.

**Victoria Mansion** — 15 minutes. An 1860 house museum, open seasonally.

## Walking and running

The **Eastern Promenade Trail** starts eight minutes east along Commercial Street and runs two
flat miles along the water to East End Beach. It is the best run in the city and the best walk if
you have an hour.

The **Western Promenade** is a steeper walk with sunset views toward the White Mountains.

## Day trips

**Freeport** — 25 minutes by car. Outlet shopping and L.L.Bean, open around the clock.

**Kennebunkport** — 40 minutes south.

**Boothbay Harbor** — 90 minutes north, a longer but prettier drive up Route 1.

## Practical

**Pharmacy** — Commercial Street, 6 minutes, open until 9:00 PM.
**Grocery** — a small market on Fore Street, 8 minutes; the nearest full supermarket needs a car.
**Urgent care** — Maine Medical Center, 10 minutes by car. In an emergency, dial 911.
**ATMs** — several on Commercial Street.

## Seasons

July and August are busy, warm, and expensive, and the Old Port is crowded every evening.
September and early October are the best weeks here — warm days, clear light, far fewer people,
and the foliage inland from mid-October. January and February are genuinely cold with harbour wind;
bring more than you think you need. Some harbour cruises do not run between November and April.
